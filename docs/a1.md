# Paper A1: soil organic matter, carbonate and transfer between regions

Paper A1 predicts **soil organic matter** (SOM, `organic_matter_g_kg`, g/kg) at the Al Moutmir
sample points from their static covariates and from percentiles of their satellite and climate time
series. It asks four questions:

- **(a) Do predicted carbonate and CEC help?** Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃, %) and cation exchange capacity
   (CEC, meq/100 g) are measured at the same points but are not available where SOM is to be
   mapped. A first model (*stage 1*) predicts them from the covariates; the SOM model (*stage 2*)
   then reads those predictions as two extra inputs.
- **(b) Does SOM behave differently by carbonate class?** The points are grouped into the four FAO
   carbonate classes - under 2 %, 2-10 %, 10-25 % and 25 % CaCO₃ or more - and the models are
   scored, and also trained, class by class.
- **(c) Does a model trained in some regions work in another?** Each of the five large hydraulic basins
   is held out in turn and predicted by a model trained elsewhere, and compared with a model
   trained on the region's own points.
- **(d) Is the loss larger where the region is more different?** The loss from (c) is set against how
   unlike its training data each held-out region is.

The code is in `src/yg_eo_soilnet/a1/`, the scripts in `experiments/a1/`, the settings in
`configs/a1/` and the tests in `tests/a1/`.

| Step | Script | What it does | Writes, under `results/a1/` |
|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | `make_plans.py` | Writes every train/test split the experiments use, once, so every model is scored on the same points (see below). | `plans/<plan>.parquet`, `plans/index.csv` |
| 3 | `run_a1.py` | For each plan: stage 1 predicts CaCO₃ and CEC; the SOM models are fitted and their test predictions saved, one row per point; for leave-one-region-out plans, how far each held-out point lies from the training data. Records everything in MLflow. | `runs/<run>/predictions/<plan>/seed_<seed>/`, `stage1/<plan>/seed_<seed>.parquet`, `dissimilarity/`, `metrics.csv`, `dissimilarity_summary.csv` |
| 4 | `evaluate.py` | Scores the saved predictions: by model, carbonate class and region, with intervals, and the transfer losses. | `runs/<run>/evaluation/` |

### The split plans

A *plan* fixes which points train a model and which score it (test). Five kinds:

| Kind | Training points | Test points | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `random` | 85 % of the points, drawn at random | the other 15 % | The baseline. |
| `spatial_kfold` | Every 10 km grid square not in the test fold | Each of 5 folds of squares in turn | How well the model does a little away from its training points. |
| `logo` | Every region but the held-out one | One whole region ("leave one region out") | (c): transfer to a region the model never saw. |
| `mixed` | Every other region, plus a share (10-90 %) of the held-out one | The rest of the held-out region | How much a little local data helps a model trained elsewhere. |
| `control` | The moved share of the held-out region alone | The same points as the matching `mixed` plan | What the other regions add, compared with local data only. |

**There is no validation set.** A model's settings (tree depth, number of trees, ...) are not chosen
on held-back validation points: they are chosen by {term}`cross-validation` *inside the training
points*. The training points are cut into 5 folds - made of whole blocks of nearby points, or
whole regions, on the spatial, leave-one-region-out and mixed plans - and each candidate setting is
scored on each fold by a model fitted on the other four. Stage 1 uses the same folds for its
out-of-fold predictions (see the leakage rule below). Since nothing is ever fitted or tuned on a
validation set, setting one aside would only take data away from training, so by default none is.
It can still be asked for - `val_size` in `plans.random` and `plans.spatial_kfold`, and
`validation_region: true` in `plans.logo` (which also covers `mixed`) - and its points are then
scored like the test points; loading such settings logs a warning saying so.

**Regions.** The regions are the 10 major hydraulic basins. Five are held out in turn - Oum Er Rbia
(2,024 points), Sebou (1,312), Tensift (1,085), Bouregreg (300) and Loukkous (290) - named in
`plans.logo.held_out`; Moulouya, Souss Massa, Daraa and Guir - Ziz - Rhris always stay in training.
A region needs at least `plans.logo.min_points` points (100) to be held out, so that its score rests
on enough points; that threshold is separate from `min_cell_size` (30), the smallest carbonate class
that gets a model of its own. Without a `held_out` list, every region with at least `min_points`
points is held out in turn - the setting to use with the finer sub-basins
(`SousBassinsHydrauliques.shp`, kept as a commented alternative in `a1.yml`). A name in the list that
is not a region of the data, or a listed region under `min_points`, stops `make_plans.py`.

**Spatial blocks.** The spatial folds are made of 10 km grid squares (`plans.spatial_kfold`,
`method: grid`, `cell_size_m: 10000`), the same size as the bootstrap's blocks. Larger blocks (the earlier 50 k-means clusters were tens of km across) push test points
further from any training point than a map pixel ever is, and make spatial CV pessimistic. To check
how much the spatial scores depend on that choice, `cell_sizes_m: [5000, 10000, 25000]` writes a
whole set of folds per size (plans `kfold_5km_f0`, `kfold_10km_f0`, ...). Each point is then tested
once per size, so the scores are always kept per size (the `block_size_m` column); `cell_size_m` is
the size the paper's spatial scores and figure 4 use.

### The models

| Model | Inputs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `global` | Every covariate | The baseline. |
| `global_aux` | Every covariate, plus stage 1's predicted CaCO₃ and CEC | Question (a). |
| `per_class_predicted` | As `global_aux`, one model per carbonate class, each point routed by its **predicted** CaCO₃ | Question (b), usable where CaCO₃ is not measured. |
| `per_class_measured` | The same, routed by **measured** CaCO₃ | A **diagnostic upper bound**, not a usable model: a new point has no measured CaCO₃. Every output marks it `model_role = diagnostic_upper_bound`. |
| `local` | As `global_aux`, trained and cross-validated inside the held-out region only, with its own stage 1 | every `logo` plan on every covariate, and on each covariate group with `--groups`; the reference for the transfer loss (see below). |

**Which models.** `stage2.models` lists the SOM models to fit (default: `global`, `global_aux`,
`per_class_measured`, `per_class_predicted`). Stage 1 is skipped for every plan when no listed
model reads its predictions - `models: [global]` - and only the folds stage 2 tunes through are cut.
`local` and `--groups` need `global_aux` among them. The evaluation and the figures use the models
the run has.

A carbonate class with fewer than `min_cell_size` (30) training points gets no model of its own; its points are
predicted by `global_aux`, and flagged `fallback = True`. With `--groups`, `global_aux` is also fitted
on each covariate group alone (satellite, terrain, climate, legacy soil maps, the two predictions),
to see which kind of input transfers best.

**The covariates.** The groups are in `configs/a1/covariate_groups.yml`:

| Group | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `satellite` | Bare-soil Sentinel-2 reflectance (`s2_*_barest`) and the percentiles of the Sentinel-2, Sentinel-1, vegetation (`AG_`) and soil (`SOIL_`) time series. Sentinel-1 columns name the polarisation first: `S1_VH_asc_q50`, `S1_VV_desc_iqr`, `S1_VH/VV_asc_q50`. |
| `terrain` | Elevation, slope, aspect, wetness index, and `landform_class` (computed from the elevation model; a category). |
| `climate` | Aridity, and the climate time-series percentiles (`CLIM_`). |
| `legacy_soil` | Layers of an existing soil map. |
| `management` | Land-management and soil-function indicators derived from the time series: carbon-use efficiency (`CUE_mean`, `CUE_cv`), trend slopes (NPP, CUE, climatic deficit, LST, soil moisture, ETa), tillage signs in the radar backscatter (`Max_VV_Spike_*`, `Tillage_Freq_*`), VH/VV ratios, radar and NDVI variability. |
| `auxiliary` | Stage 1's predicted CaCO₃ and CEC. |

In `legacy_soil`, `soc_median_0_30cm` is an **external estimate of soil organic carbon** - a prior on
the target itself. Read the `legacy_soil` scores with that in mind: part of what the group adds is an
earlier estimate of SOM. (`som_median_0_30cm`, the same map in organic-matter units, is not in the
current static file.)

**The time-series percentiles** are either already columns of the static file
(`timeseries_percentiles.source: static`, the real data's case) - `<variable>_q10`, `_q25`, `_q50`,
`_q75`, `_q90` and `<variable>_iqr`, e.g. `S2_B12_q50`, `CLIM_ETa_iqr` - or computed by A1 from a
time-series file (`source: compute`), as `ts_<variable>_p<percentile>` columns plus `ts_n_obs`. With
`static`, no time series is read: `data.timeseries` is null and the main configuration has
`temporal.enabled: false`.

**Never inputs.** `exclude_inputs` lists column-name prefixes removed from the inputs whatever the
source - by default `S2_SZA` (the Sentinel-2 sun zenith angle) and `S1_angle` (the Sentinel-1
incidence angle): acquisition geometry describes the satellite, not the soil. A prefix catches every
column starting with it (`S2_SZA_q50`, `S1_angle_asc_iqr`, and the computed `ts_S2_SZA_p50`); how
many were removed is logged. Lab values are never inputs either: they are listed in the data spec's
`LABEL_COLUMNS`. Both rules are checked whenever the table is built - by `run_a1.py` and by the
pre-flight checks - and a lab value or an excluded column among the inputs stops the run.

## The leakage rule

{term}`Leakage` is a model being scored on points whose answers it saw while being built. Two
stages make it easy to introduce by accident: if stage 1 saw a point's measured CaCO₃, the CaCO₃
prediction handed to stage 2 for that point would be better than any prediction a new point could
get, and stage 2 would learn to trust it too much. The rules, each enforced by a test:

1. **Stage 1 is recomputed for every plan**, from that plan's training points only. A test point's
   CaCO₃ and CEC never enter any model of its own plan.
2. **Training points are cross-fitted.** Stage 2 is trained on stage 1's predictions for the
   training points, so those must be as good - and no better - than for a new point. The training
   points are cut into 5 inner folds, and each fold's predictions come from a stage-1 model fitted
   on the other four: no training point's prediction comes from a model that saw its measured
   value. On spatial, leave-one-region-out and mixed plans the folds are made of whole blocks or
   regions, so a point is not predicted by a model trained on its near neighbours either.
3. **Test points are predicted by the stage-1 model fitted on all the training points** - the same
   model a new point would get.
4. **Stage 2 is tuned and fitted on the training points only**, choosing its settings by
   {term}`cross-validation` over the same inner folds. Test points are only scored.
5. **The `local` model follows the same rules inside the region.** Its outer folds are whole
   spatial blocks of the region; for each fold, stage 1 is refitted on the region's other folds
   (cross-fitted by block for their points) - never the leave-one-region-out plan's stage 1, which
   was fitted on the other regions - and stage 2 is tuned on the same grid as the transferred model.

`tests/a1/test_a1_auxiliary.py` checks rules 1-3 with a *spy*: a stand-in model that records every
point it is fitted on, and fails the test if any prediction came from a model that saw that point.
`tests/a1/test_a1_run.py` checks rule 4 and that `global` never reads the stage-1 predictions.
`tests/a1/test_a1_local.py` checks rule 5 the same way, in both stages, for points and their blocks.

## Reading the results

**Intervals.** Every score comes with a 95 % interval from a {term}`bootstrap` that resamples whole
10 km squares of points rather than single points (`bootstrap.block_size_km`). Nearby samples are
alike, so resampling them one by one would treat near-copies as independent evidence and make the
intervals too narrow. Two models are compared on the same points, resampled together, which gives
an interval and a p-value for their difference. When many differences are read together, their
p-values are corrected with **Holm's method**, which raises each just enough that the chance of any
false finding in the family stays at 5 %.

**Transfer loss.** For a `logo` plan, the *drop* is the held-out region's RMSE when trained elsewhere
minus its RMSE when trained on its own points (`local`): positive means transfer does worse. For a
`mixed` plan it is compared with its `control` plan instead.

**Mixed and control plans are never pooled.** There is one of each per region, share and repeat, and
each tests the region's remaining points, so pooled they would count one point many times (99,760
rows from 3,661 points on the real data). `evaluate.py` scores them per region and share instead,
averaged over the repeats (`scores_mixed.csv`), and leaves them out of `scores.csv`,
`scores_by_class.csv` and `intervals.csv`. Within a repeat, a `mixed` plan and its `control` plan
test the same points, so their difference is a **paired** comparison: `differences.csv` holds it in
rows of kind `mixed_vs_control` (mixed minus control, per region, share and model, a block bootstrap
on the shared points, Holm-corrected across the shares of one region and model).

The `local` model is built to be as fair a reference as the transferred one, not a flattering one:

- **Outer folds of whole blocks.** The region's points are placed in squares of
  `bootstrap.block_size_km` (10 km), and the squares packed into `inner_cv_folds` (5) folds - fewer
  when the region has fewer squares. Each fold is predicted by a model fitted on the others, so a
  point is never predicted from its neighbours. Each local point's fold (`local_fold`) and square
  (`local_block`) are saved with its prediction.
- **Its own stage 1.** For each fold, stage 1 is refitted on the region's other folds, so the
  predicted CaCO3 and CEC it reads are local too.
- **Tuned.** Stage 2 is tuned on the same settings grid as the transferred model, through folds of
  whole squares inside the training folds.
- **Enough squares.** A region needs at least 3 squares; with fewer, every outer training fold
  would hold a single square and could neither be cross-fitted nor tuned by square. Such a region
  gets no local score, and `run_a1.py` says why.

**How different a region is.** Three measures, per held-out point and summarised per region:

- The **dissimilarity index** (DI; Meyer & Pebesma, 2021): the distance from a point to its nearest
  training point, in the model's inputs - each standardised, and weighted by how much the model
  relies on it (its feature importance) - divided by the average distance between two training
  points. 0 on top of a training point; about 1 as far away as training points are from each other.
- The **area of applicability**: a point whose DI is larger than any training point reached during
  cross-validation (leaving out outliers) is *outside* it - the model is extrapolating there.
- **MESS** (multivariate environmental similarity surface; Elith et al., 2010): for each input, how
  far inside (positive) or outside (negative) the training range a point lies, and which input is
  furthest out.

Categorical inputs are left out of all three: distances and ranges are not defined for categories.
The summary also gives each region's carbonate-class mix against training's (the *total-variation
distance*: 0 for the same mix, 1 for no class in common) and how much the SOM-CEC and SOM-CaCO₃
correlations (Spearman) differ from training's.

## Seeds: repeating the fits

`seeds` in the settings lists the random seeds (`[42]` by default; `configs/a1/a1_xgb.yml` has
`[42, 7, 2026]`).

- **The first seed alone builds the plans** - the splits, the blocks, the mixed draws. Adding a
  seed never changes a plan or its hash, so a seed can be added to a run that has already started:
  the run folder takes it, and only the new seed is fitted.
- **Every seed repeats the model fits on those plans**: the folds stage 1 cross-fits through (which
  are also the folds stage 2 chooses its settings with), the `local` model's folds, and the
  `random_state` of every estimator. The first seed's folds are the ones every run before seeds
  were repeated used; each further seed deals the points - or, on spatial and regional plans,
  whole blocks and regions - into other folds.
- **Outputs per seed**: `predictions/<plan>/seed_<seed>/` and `stage1/<plan>/seed_<seed>.parquet`;
  every prediction row has a `seed` column. `manifest.json` records the seeds done per plan, and a
  run resumes per plan and seed. `run_a1.py --seeds 42,7` runs some of the seeds.
- **The evaluation**: every table of `evaluate.py` has a `seed` column. The primary result is the
  first seed, with its block-bootstrap intervals; `seed_summary.csv` gives each headline metric's
  mean, SD, min and max over the seeds, and `differences.csv` how many seeds give each model
  difference the sign the first seed gives it (`n_seeds_same_sign` of `n_seeds`). The figures show
  the first seed.

**Where the spread comes from.** XGBoost with `tree_method: hist` and no subsampling (no
`subsample` or `colsample_*` below 1) builds the same trees from the same data whatever its seed.
The spread between seeds therefore comes from the fold assignments alone: which settings the
search picks, and which fits predict each training point's CaCO₃ and CEC in stage 1. A model fitted
on every training point with a single setting to try (`global`, untuned) gives the same predictions
under every seed.

## The figures

`make_figures.py` saves each figure as PNG and PDF in `runs/<run>/figures/`, 90 or 190 mm wide
(one or two journal columns) with no text under 7 pt. Beside them:

- `data/<name>.csv` - every number a figure plots, so a value in the text can be traced to it;
  `data/table_rpiq_by_class` and `data/table_basins` also as `.tex`, ready for the paper;
- `captions.md` - a draft caption per figure, with the numbers read from those tables. Edit them;
  they are rewritten each time the figures are drawn.

A figure is drawn only when the run holds its inputs; otherwise it is saved as a short note,
named after the figure, that says what this run lacks and what to run. A run of
`--kinds logo,mixed`, for instance, gets notes for figure 4 (no random split), figure 5 (it compares
designs and needs two) and figure S1 (no block sizes beyond those of figure 6). Captions and the
schematic of figure 1 describe only the designs the run has. Without a random split, figures 11 and
S4 explain the global model of each leave-one-basin-out plan (one panel per held-out basin) and say
so.

## Run the demo

```bash
pixi run -e dev a1-demo
```

It makes a made-up 1,200-point dataset (`experiments/a1/demo_data/`) and runs every step on it in a
few minutes, in **dev mode**: random plans, spatial folds of 10 km and 25 km grid squares, and two
of the four demo regions held out (`configs/a1/a1_demo.yml`); XGBoost in both stages, 50 trees, no

demo leaves out to stay quick.

## Run it on the real data

The real settings are `configs/a1/a1.yml`, with `configs/a1/main_config.yml` (a copy of the main
configuration whose data spec makes the soil-map layers inputs) and `configs/a1/covariate_groups.yml`.

1. **Check the paths.** In `a1.yml`, the `data:` block names the data folder and two files - the
   static file, which holds the covariates, the time-series percentiles, the lab values and the
   coordinates (`Morocco_roadsafe_6k_with_ts_features_and_covariates_V_310826.csv`), and the targets
   file (`Morocco_soil_targets_uuid_roadsafe_6ksample_V_310826.csv`) - with `timeseries: null`. They
   must match `configs/a1/main_config.yml`, which also has `temporal.enabled: false`; loading stops
   if the two disagree. `timeseries_percentiles.source` is `static`, and there is no sampling year
   (`sample_year.column: null`). The regions are the major hydraulic basins, five of them held out:

   ```yaml
   regions:
     source: polygons
     polygons_path: /home/kmisbah/CESBIO/AL_Moutmir/BDMaroc/BassinsHydrauliques.shp
     name_field: Bassin
   plans:
     logo:
       min_points: 100
       held_out: [Oum Er Rbia, Sebou, Tensift, Loukkous, Bouregreg]
   ```

   (`source: column` with `column: <name>` uses a column of the data instead.)

2. **Check the data:**

   ```bash
   python experiments/a1/preflight.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml
   ```

   Read `results/a1/preflight/`: every input with its covariate group (`inputs.csv`) and the inputs
   no group claims, which no model would read; columns with many gaps; regions and region x class
   cells with fewer than 30 points; and, when the data has them, whether earlier CaCO₃/CEC
   predictions (`*_baseline`) look over-fitted. Preflight stops if a lab value or an excluded
   column would be an input.

3. **Write the plans** - about 460 with the five held-out basins (461 plans, 56 MB, written in
   under a minute on the earlier 5,295-point file; the size grows with the number of points):

   ```bash
   python experiments/a1/make_plans.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml
   ```

4. **Run the models.** Stage 1 is {term}`TabICL` in the real settings, which is slow on a CPU: it is
   fitted again for every plan and every inner fold. Start with the plans the paper's main tables need,
   then add the mixed plans:

   ```bash
   python experiments/a1/run_a1.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml --kinds random,spatial_kfold,logo --groups
   python experiments/a1/run_a1.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml --kinds mixed --groups
   ```

   `configs/a1/a1_xgb.yml` is the same experiment with XGBoost in both stages (stage 2 on the
   graphics card) and three seeds, into `results/a1_xgb`; the commands are at the top of the file.

   A run **resumes**: stop it at any point and run the same command again, and only what is missing
   is computed. A run folder made from other settings or other plans is refused, so two experiments
   never mix; `--restart` clears it instead (as does a folder written by an older version of
   `run_a1.py`). Watch the run in MLflow (`pixi run mlflow`, experiment `Paper_A1`).

   Plans written before validation sets were dropped hold validation points; write them again
   (step 3), then run with `--restart` - the old run folders are refused, as they should be.

5. **Score it, and draw the figures:**

   ```bash
   python experiments/a1/evaluate.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml
   python experiments/a1/make_figures.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml
   ```

   or open `notebooks/a1_figures.ipynb` with the `explore` environment (`pixi run -e explore
   notebook`) and set `SETTINGS = "configs/a1/a1.yml"` and `REGIONS = None` at the top.

## Speed: workers and the graphics card

**Where the time goes.** One mixed plan of the real data with every model (stage 1 untuned,
stage 2 searched over 10 settings with 5 folds) took 23 minutes on the processor (an i7-9850H,
6 cores, 2 threads each). 96 % of it is XGBoost growing trees: the two per-class models 69 % (each
class searched on its own), `global` and `global_aux` 28 %, stage 1 3 %, predicting under 2 %. The
search fits 10 settings x 5 folds plus the final fit for each model - 67,100 boosting rounds for
the plan.

**The graphics card** (`stage2.init_args.device: cuda`, as in `configs/a1/a1_xgb.yml`) ran the
same plan in 14 minutes - stage 1 still on the processor - with the same RMSE for every model to
four decimals and the same settings chosen. XGBoost built without CUDA, or finding no card, would
silently fit on the processor; `run_a1.py` fits a tiny model on the card first and stops with a
message if it cannot. `run_a1.py --device cpu|cuda` puts both stages on one processor (the demo,
the tests and CI stay on `cpu`). Before predicting, each fitted model is moved to the processor,
where the data are. Two targets in one model (`multi_strategy: multi_output_tree`) do train on the
card in the installed XGBoost (3.3), 5x faster than on the processor; stage 1 stays on `cpu` in the
configurations, as it is 3 % of a plan.

**Workers.** `--workers N` runs plans and seeds in `N` processes, each estimator with
`cpu_count // N` threads; the predictions are identical to one process (checked on the demo and on
real plans, on the processor and the card). On this 6-core machine the gain is modest - 12 threads
already oversubscribe 6 cores (one fit: 7.8 s with 12 threads, 4.0 s with 6) - so 10 real plans
went from 193 s to 170 s with 4 workers on the processor, and from 131 s to 111 s with 3 on the
card. One process on the card holds about 550 MiB of its memory (4 GB on this machine).
Recommended: `--workers 2` on the processor; `--workers 2` or `3` with the card - more fit in its
memory, but they share its computing and the processor still prepares the data and fits stage 1.

## Watching a run

Every script reports its progress on the console as it goes, and appends the same lines to a log
file, so a run stopped half-way keeps its history:

| Script | Log file |
|---|---|
| `run_a1.py` | `results/a1/runs/<run>/run_a1.log` |
| `preflight.py` | `results/a1/preflight/preflight.log` |
| `make_plans.py` | `results/a1/plans/make_plans.log` |
| `evaluate.py` | `results/a1/runs/<run>/evaluation/evaluate.log` |
| `make_figures.py` | `results/a1/runs/<run>/figures/make_figures.log` |

To follow a long run from another terminal: `tail -f results/a1/runs/default/run_a1.log`.
`run_a1.py` writes one line when each plan starts, one for its stage 1 (time taken, and the
out-of-fold R² of CaCO₃ and CEC), one for each model and covariate group (time taken and test RMSE,
or "already done" for a resumed one), one per region and fold of the local model, and one when the
plan and seed ends, with an estimate of the time left. With `--workers`, only that last line is
shown per plan and seed (the workers' own lines go to the log file):

```text
[3/26] kfold_10km_f2 seed 42 (spatial_kfold): 4,644 train / 1,162 test
  stage 1: 41.2 s; out-of-fold R2 caco3_pct_total 0.58, c_e_c_meq_100g 0.62
  global / combined: 12.3 s, test RMSE 8.10
  ...
[3/26] kfold_10km_f2 seed 42 done in 2m14s; global test RMSE 8.10; about 41m left (2m03s per plan over 3 computed)
```

Progress and warnings go to standard error, the end summary to standard output. `--quiet` keeps only
the warnings and the summary on the console; the log file still gets every line. A `--restart`
clears the run folder but keeps `run_a1.log`. At the end of a run - finished or failed - the log is
added to the run's MLflow parent run as an artifact, and a failed run is marked `FAILED` there.

## Where the outputs appear in MLflow

Each run folder (`results/a1/runs/<run name>/`) is one **parent run** in MLflow, in the experiment
`mlflow_experiment` names (`Paper_A1`; `Paper_A1_Demo` for the demo). `run_a1.py` opens it and writes
its id into the folder's `manifest.json`; running `run_a1.py` again on the same folder reopens the
same parent, and so do `evaluate.py` and `make_figures.py`, which add their outputs to it.
`--restart` starts the folder, and its parent run, afresh.

| What | Where in MLflow | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| Settings, git commit, kinds, region and block-size choices, the SOM models and each stage's device | parent run, *Parameters* (`a1_*`, `a1_models`, `a1_device_stage1`, `a1_device_stage2`) | `run_a1.py` |
| Which models are diagnostics | parent run, tags `model_role` (each model's role) and `a1_diagnostic_models` | `run_a1.py` |
| The settings file, `metrics.csv`, `manifest.json`, `dissimilarity_summary.csv` | parent run, *Artifacts* | `run_a1.py` |
| Each plan's scores on its test points, per seed | one child run per plan and seed, `<plan>__seed_<seed>` (`plan_id`, `kind`, `held_out`, `seed` tags; `seed`, `device_stage1`, `device_stage2` parameters), metrics `rmse_test__<model>__<group>`, ... | `run_a1.py` |
| The evaluation tables (`scores.csv`, `intervals.csv`, `intervals_by_region.csv`, `differences.csv`, `transfer.csv`, `scores_mixed.csv`, `seed_summary.csv`, ...) and `region_names.json` | parent run, *Artifacts* `evaluation/` | `evaluate.py` |
| Headline scores | parent run, *Metrics* (below) | `evaluate.py` |
| The figures | parent run, *Artifacts* `figures/` - PDF files, PNGs that preview in the browser, `captions.md`, and `data/` (each figure's numbers as CSV, the paper tables also as LaTeX) | `make_figures.py` |

**Headline metric names** are `<score>__<plans>__<model>[__<group>]`, all on test points, for
`rmse`, `mae`, `r2`, `bias` and `rpiq`:

- `<plans>` is `random`; `spatial_kfold_<size>m` - every fold of one block size pooled, e.g.
  `spatial_kfold_10000m`; `logo_<region>` - one held-out region; or `logo_mean`, the mean over the
  regions. Region names are written in lower-case letters, digits and underscores
  (`logo_oum_er_rbia`); `evaluation/region_names.json` and the parent's `region__<name>` tags give
  each one's real name.
- `__<group>` is added for a model fitted on one covariate group alone
  (`rmse__random__global_aux__terrain`) and left out for every covariate.
- `rmse__mixed_<region>_f<share>__<model>` and `rmse__control_<region>_f<share>__<model>` score
  the mixed and control plans of one region and share, averaged over the repeats
  (`rmse__mixed_oum_er_rbia_f0_1__global`); they are never pooled across shares.
- `rmse_drop__logo_<region>__<group>` is the transfer drop (`rmse_drop__logo_mean__<group>` its
  mean), and `rmse__...__lo` / `__hi` (also for `r2`) the 95 % bootstrap interval of a score on
  every covariate.
- These are the first seed's. A run of several seeds adds `<metric>__..._sd`, the SD of each over
  the seeds (`rmse__random__global_sd`).

With `--no-mlflow`, a script writes its files only. `evaluate.py` and `make_figures.py` without it
stop at once if the folder records no MLflow run (it was made with `--no-mlflow`) or MLflow no
longer has that run.

### `run_a1.py`

| Option | Default | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| `--config` | `configs/a1/a1.yml` | The A1 settings file. |
| `--plans` | `<output_dir>/plans` | The folder `make_plans.py` wrote. |
| `--kinds` | all | Comma-separated plan kinds to run; `mixed` brings its `control` plans. |
| `--groups` | off | Also fit `global_aux`, and the `local` model of each leave-one-region-out plan, on each covariate group alone. The `local` model on every covariate is fitted without it. |
| `--only` | all | Comma-separated `plan_id` patterns (`"random,kfold_10km_*,logo_*"`): only the plans matching one, on top of `--kinds`. Limits `--groups` to some plans. |
| `--seeds` | all of `seeds` | Comma-separated seeds to run, among the settings' `seeds`. |
| `--workers` | 1 | Plans and seeds fitted side by side in this many processes; each estimator gets `cpu_count // workers` threads. The predictions are the same as with 1. |
| `--device` | the settings' | `cpu` or `cuda`: the processor of both stages, replacing the settings'. A stage on `cuda` is checked first: the run stops if XGBoost cannot use the graphics card. |
| `--run-name` | `default` | The run folder, under `<output_dir>/runs/`. |
| `--restart` | off | Clear a run folder made from other settings or plans, instead of refusing to add to it. |
| `--no-mlflow` | off | Do not record the run in MLflow. |
| `--dev-mode` | off | The quickest run through every step: XGBoost in both stages, 50 trees, untuned, 2 inner folds. Its scores mean nothing. |
| `--quiet` | off | Show only warnings and the end summary on the console; the log file still records every line. |
