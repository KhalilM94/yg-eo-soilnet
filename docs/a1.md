# Paper A1: soil organic matter, carbonate and transfer between regions

Paper A1 predicts **soil organic matter** (SOM, `organic_matter_g_kg`, g/kg) at the Al Moutmir
sample points from their static covariates and from percentiles of their satellite and climate time
series. It asks four questions:

- **(a) Do predicted carbonate and CEC help?** Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃, %) and cation exchange capacity
   (CEC, meq/100 g) are measured at the same points but are not available where SOM is to be
   mapped. A first model (*stage 1*) predicts them from the covariates; the SOM model (*stage 2*)
   then reads those predictions as two extra inputs.
- **(b) Does SOM behave differently by carbonate class?** The points are grouped into the four FAO
   carbonate classes - under 2 %, 2-10 %, 10-25 % and 25 % CaCO₃ or more - and the models are
   scored, and also trained, class by class.
- **(c) Does a model trained in some regions work in another?** Each of the five large hydraulic basins
   is held out in turn and predicted by a model trained elsewhere, and compared with a model
   trained on the region's own points.
- **(d) Is the loss larger where the region is more different?** The loss from (c) is set against how
   unlike its training data each held-out region is.

The code is in `src/yg_eo_soilnet/a1/`, the scripts in `experiments/a1/`, the settings in
`configs/a1/` and the tests in `tests/a1/`.

### The split plans

A *plan* fixes which points train a model and which score it (test). Five kinds, and two arms of
the mixed experiment (below):

| Kind | Training points | Test points | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `random` | 85 % of the points, drawn at random | the other 15 % | The baseline. |
| `spatial_kfold` | Every 10 km grid square not in the test fold | Each of 5 folds of squares in turn | How well the model does a little away from its training points. |
| `logo` | Every region but the held-out one | One whole region ("leave one region out") | (c): transfer to a region the model never saw. |
| `mixed` | Every other region, plus a share (10-90 %) of the held-out one | The rest of the held-out region | How much a little local data helps a model trained elsewhere. |
| `control` | The moved share of the held-out region alone | The same points as the matching `mixed` plan | What the other regions add, compared with local data only. |
| `mixed_weighted` | The points of the matching `mixed` plan, the moved ones weighing more | The same points as the matching `mixed` plan | Do local points help more when they count for more? |
| `mixed_indicator` | The points of the matching `mixed` plan, with an input saying which are from the held-out region | The same points as the matching `mixed` plan | Does it help that the model knows which points are local? |
| `logo`, matched (`logo_m<size>_…`) | About `plans.logo.training_size` points of the other regions, drawn as whole 10 km squares | One whole region, as in plain `logo` | Is a region hard to transfer to because it is different, or only because holding it out leaves less to learn from? |

**The arms of the mixed experiment.** `plans.mixed.arms: [weighted, indicator]` writes, beside each
`mixed` plan, a `mixed_w_<rest of its id>` and a `mixed_i_<rest of its id>` plan with exactly its
points: the same moved points, the same test points. Only the training changes:

- **weighted**: in every fit - the final one, each fit of the settings search, and stage 1 when
  it runs - each moved point weighs `w` and every other training point 1. With
  `plans.mixed.local_weight: balanced` (the default), `w` = (training points from the other
  regions) / (moved points), counted over the training points with a measured SOM, so the moved
  points and the rest carry the same total weight; a number sets `w` directly. The search still
  picks its settings by the plain, unweighted RMSE of each validation fold. `w` is recorded in the
  `local_weight` column of every predictions row of the plan (empty for other kinds) and in the
  plan's log line.
- **indicator**: one more input, `a1_in_target_basin`: 1 for every point of the held-out region
  (moved and test), 0 elsewhere. Only the models on every covariate (`combined`: `global`,
  `global_aux`, the per-class models) read it; the per-group models (`--groups`) and stage 1 do not.

The arms add no random draw, so the `mixed` and `control` plans are the same files with or without
them. `experiments/a1/check_plans.py <plans A> <plans B>` checks this between two plan folders: for
every plan id in both, it compares the files byte for byte and the test points, prints a table of
the plans that differ, and exits with code 1 if any does. Run it before comparing two
configurations plan by plan, as `configs/a1/a1_mixed_arms.yml` does against `results/a1_mixed`.
The arms run like any kind: `run_a1.py --kinds mixed,control,mixed_weighted,mixed_indicator`.

**Leave one region out with the training size matched.** Holding out Oum Er Rbia removes 37 % of
the training points and Sebou 26 %, so a poor `logo` score there mixes "this basin is different"
with "less training data". `plans.logo.training_size` cuts every `logo` plan's training set to about
that many points (`configs/a1/a1_logo_matched.yml`: 3,600, about what is left when Oum Er Rbia is
held out):

- The training points are drawn as whole squares of `bootstrap.block_size_km` (10 km, the grid the
  intervals resample in), in a random order, until the size is reached, so it is missed by less
  than one square. The other regions' points not drawn are left out of the plan: neither trained
  nor tested on.
- The test set is the whole held-out region, as in the plain plan, and the local model is the
  same: it only uses the held-out region.
- `plans.logo.n_repeats` (default 1) draws per region, each seeded by the first seed, the repeat
  and the region, so a rebuilt plan draws the same squares. Ids are
  `logo_m<size>_<region>_s<repeat>`, repeats from 0, e.g. `logo_m3600_r06_oum_er_rbia_s0`; the kind
  stays `logo`, and the `repeat` column tells the draws apart.
- A region whose training set is no larger than `training_size` keeps all of it, with a warning.
- `make_plans.py` logs each plan's size (asked, drawn, squares, points left out), and `index.csv`
  gains `training_size` (asked) and `n_left_out`; `n_train` is the size drawn.
- Without `training_size` nothing changes: the plain `logo` plans keep their ids and files, and
  `index.csv` its columns.

On the real data, the 25 plans of `a1_logo_matched.yml` hold 3,583 to 3,637 training points. Holding
out Oum Er Rbia leaves 3,660, so its five draws differ by only about 60 points and spread little;
Bouregreg and Loukkous leave about 1,900 points out of each draw. `evaluate.py` scores matched plans
separately: each draw is scored on its own and summarised per basin - mean and min-max over the
draws - in `scores_logo_matched.csv`, and the draws are left out of every pooled table, so no basin's
test points count five times (see *Reading the results*).

**There is no validation set.** A model's settings (tree depth, number of trees, ...) are not chosen
on held-back validation points: they are chosen by {term}`cross-validation` *inside the training
points*. The training points are cut into 5 folds - made of whole blocks of nearby points, or
whole regions, on the spatial, leave-one-region-out and mixed plans - and each candidate setting is
scored on each fold by a model fitted on the other four. Stage 1 uses the same folds for its
out-of-fold predictions (see the leakage rule below). Since nothing is ever fitted or tuned on a
validation set, setting one aside would only take data away from training, so by default none is.
It can still be asked for - `val_size` in `plans.random` and `plans.spatial_kfold`, and
`validation_region: true` in `plans.logo` (which also covers `mixed`) - and its points are then
scored like the test points; loading such settings logs a warning saying so.

**Regions.** The regions are the 10 major hydraulic basins. Five are held out in turn - Oum Er Rbia
(2,024 points), Sebou (1,312), Tensift (1,085), Bouregreg (300) and Loukkous (290) - named in
`plans.logo.held_out`; Moulouya, Souss Massa, Daraa and Guir - Ziz - Rhris always stay in training.
A region needs at least `plans.logo.min_points` points (100) to be held out, so that its score rests
on enough points; that threshold is separate from `min_cell_size` (30), the smallest carbonate class
that gets a model of its own. Without a `held_out` list, every region with at least `min_points`
points is held out in turn - the setting to use with the finer sub-basins
(`SousBassinsHydrauliques.shp`, kept as a commented alternative in `a1.yml`). A name in the list that
is not a region of the data, or a listed region under `min_points`, stops `make_plans.py`.

**Spatial blocks.** The spatial folds are made of 10 km grid squares (`plans.spatial_kfold`,
`method: grid`, `cell_size_m: 10000`), the same size as the bootstrap's blocks. Larger blocks (the earlier 50 k-means clusters were tens of km across) push test points
further from any training point than a map pixel ever is, and make spatial CV pessimistic. To check
how much the spatial scores depend on that choice, `cell_sizes_m: [5000, 10000, 25000]` writes a
whole set of folds per size (plans `kfold_5km_f0`, `kfold_10km_f0`, ...). Each point is then tested
once per size, so the scores are always kept per size (the `block_size_m` column); `cell_size_m` is
the size the paper's spatial scores and figure 4 use.

### The models

| Model | Inputs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `global` | Every covariate | The baseline. |
| `global_aux` | Every covariate, plus stage 1's predicted CaCO₃ and CEC | Question (a). |
| `per_class_predicted` | As `global_aux`, one model per carbonate class, each point routed by its **predicted** CaCO₃ | Question (b), usable where CaCO₃ is not measured. |
| `per_class_measured` | The same, routed by **measured** CaCO₃ | A **diagnostic upper bound**, not a usable model: a new point has no measured CaCO₃. Every output marks it `model_role = diagnostic_upper_bound`. |
| `local_global` | As `global` (every covariate, no predicted CaCO₃/CEC), trained and cross-validated inside the held-out region only, on the same outer folds as `local` | every `logo` plan when `global` is fitted; Global's reference for the transfer loss, the one every figure and table reports (see below). |
| `local` | As `global_aux`, trained and cross-validated inside the held-out region only, with its own stage 1 | every `logo` plan on every covariate, and on each covariate group with `--groups`; the reference for `global_aux` and for each group alone. |

**Which models.** `stage2.models` lists the SOM models to fit (default: `global`, `global_aux`,
`per_class_measured`, `per_class_predicted`). Stage 1 is skipped for every plan when no listed
model reads its predictions - `models: [global]` - and only the folds stage 2 tunes through are cut.
`local` and `--groups` need `global_aux` among them; `local_global` needs `global`. The evaluation
and the figures use the models the run has. A run made before `local_global` existed gets it by
running `run_a1.py` again with the same settings: it resumes, and fits only the missing files.

A carbonate class with fewer than `min_cell_size` (30) training points gets no model of its own; its points are
predicted by `global_aux`, and flagged `fallback = True`. With `--groups`, `global_aux` is also fitted
on each covariate group alone (satellite, terrain, climate, legacy soil maps, the two predictions),
to see which kind of input transfers best.

**The covariates.** The groups are in `configs/a1/covariate_groups.yml`:

| Group | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `satellite` | Bare-soil Sentinel-2 reflectance (`s2_*_barest`) and the percentiles of the Sentinel-2, Sentinel-1, vegetation (`AG_`) and soil (`SOIL_`) time series. Sentinel-1 columns name the polarisation first: `S1_VH_asc_q50`, `S1_VV_desc_iqr`, `S1_VH/VV_asc_q50`. |
| `terrain` | Elevation, slope, aspect, wetness index, and `landform_class` (computed from the elevation model; a category). |
| `climate` | Aridity, and the climate time-series percentiles (`CLIM_`). |
| `legacy_soil` | Layers of an existing soil map. |
| `management` | Land-management and soil-function indicators derived from the time series: carbon-use efficiency (`CUE_mean`, `CUE_cv`), trend slopes (NPP, CUE, climatic deficit, LST, soil moisture, ETa), tillage signs in the radar backscatter (`Max_VV_Spike_*`, `Tillage_Freq_*`), VH/VV ratios, radar and NDVI variability. |
| `auxiliary` | Stage 1's predicted CaCO₃ and CEC. |

In `legacy_soil`, `soc_median_0_30cm` is an **external estimate of soil organic carbon** - a prior on
the target itself. Read the `legacy_soil` scores with that in mind: part of what the group adds is an
earlier estimate of SOM. (`som_median_0_30cm`, the same map in organic-matter units, is not in the
current static file.)

**The time-series percentiles** are either already columns of the static file
(`timeseries_percentiles.source: static`, the real data's case) - `<variable>_q10`, `_q25`, `_q50`,
`_q75`, `_q90` and `<variable>_iqr`, e.g. `S2_B12_q50`, `CLIM_ETa_iqr` - or computed by A1 from a
time-series file (`source: compute`), as `ts_<variable>_p<percentile>` columns plus `ts_n_obs`. With
`static`, no time series is read: `data.timeseries` is null and the main configuration has
`temporal.enabled: false`.

**Never inputs.** `exclude_inputs` lists column-name prefixes removed from the inputs whatever the
source - by default `S2_SZA` (the Sentinel-2 sun zenith angle) and `S1_angle` (the Sentinel-1
incidence angle): acquisition geometry describes the satellite, not the soil. A prefix catches every
column starting with it (`S2_SZA_q50`, `S1_angle_asc_iqr`, and the computed `ts_S2_SZA_p50`); how
many were removed is logged. Lab values are never inputs either: they are listed in the data spec's
`LABEL_COLUMNS`. Both rules are checked whenever the table is built - by `run_a1.py` and by the
pre-flight checks - and a lab value or an excluded column among the inputs stops the run.

## The leakage rule

{term}`Leakage` is a model being scored on points whose answers it saw while being built. Two
stages make it easy to introduce by accident: if stage 1 saw a point's measured CaCO₃, the CaCO₃
prediction handed to stage 2 for that point would be better than any prediction a new point could
get, and stage 2 would learn to trust it too much. The rules, each enforced by a test:

1. **Stage 1 is recomputed for every plan**, from that plan's training points only. A test point's
   CaCO₃ and CEC never enter any model of its own plan.
2. **Training points are cross-fitted.** Stage 2 is trained on stage 1's predictions for the
   training points, so those must be as good - and no better - than for a new point. The training
   points are cut into 5 inner folds, and each fold's predictions come from a stage-1 model fitted
   on the other four: no training point's prediction comes from a model that saw its measured
   value. On spatial, leave-one-region-out and mixed plans the folds are made of whole blocks or
   regions, so a point is not predicted by a model trained on its near neighbours either.
3. **Test points are predicted by the stage-1 model fitted on all the training points** - the same
   model a new point would get.
4. **Stage 2 is tuned and fitted on the training points only**, choosing its settings by
   {term}`cross-validation` over the same inner folds. Test points are only scored.
5. **The `local` model follows the same rules inside the region.** Its outer folds are whole
   spatial blocks of the region; for each fold, stage 1 is refitted on the region's other folds
   (cross-fitted by block for their points) - never the leave-one-region-out plan's stage 1, which
   was fitted on the other regions - and stage 2 is tuned on the same grid as the transferred model.

`tests/a1/test_a1_auxiliary.py` checks rules 1-3 with a *spy*: a stand-in model that records every
point it is fitted on, and fails the test if any prediction came from a model that saw that point.
`tests/a1/test_a1_run.py` checks rule 4 and that `global` never reads the stage-1 predictions.
`tests/a1/test_a1_local.py` checks rule 5 the same way, in both stages, for points and their blocks.

## Reading the results

**Intervals.** Every score comes with a 95 % interval from a {term}`bootstrap` that resamples whole
10 km squares of points rather than single points (`bootstrap.block_size_km`). Nearby samples are
alike, so resampling them one by one would treat near-copies as independent evidence and make the
intervals too narrow. Two models are compared on the same points, resampled together, which gives
an interval and a p-value for their difference. When many differences are read together, their
p-values are corrected with **Holm's method**, which raises each just enough that the chance of any
false finding in the family stays at 5 %.

**Transfer loss.** For a `logo` plan, the *drop* is the held-out region's RMSE when trained elsewhere
minus its RMSE when trained on its own points: positive means transfer does worse. For a `mixed`
plan it is compared with its `control` plan instead. The two models always read the same inputs.
`transfer.csv` says which pair a row compares in its `inputs` column:

| `inputs` | Transferred | Local | Read by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `every covariate` | `global` | `local_global` | every figure, table and headline number (figures 8-10, S5, S6, C1) |
| `with predicted CaCO3/CEC` | `global_aux` | `local` | kept for reference; no figure |
| `covariate group alone` | `global_aux` on one group | `local` on the same group | figure 8b (`--groups`) |

Without `local_global` (a run made before it existed) the local columns of the main rows are
empty, and the figures say so rather than fall back to the other pair.

**Debiased RMSE.** sqrt(RMSE² − bias²), the spread of the errors around their mean: what the RMSE
would be once a constant offset is removed. `intervals.csv` and `intervals_by_region.csv` give it
an interval like every other score (`rmse_debiased`), and `basin_errors.csv` (table S6) sets
Global's transferred RMSE, bias and debiased RMSE beside the local RMSE, per basin. When the
debiased RMSE comes close to the local one, transfer loses mostly through a shift.

**Mixed, arm and control plans are never pooled.** There is one of each per region, share and repeat, and
each tests the region's remaining points, so pooled they would count one point many times (99,760
rows from 3,661 points on the real data). `evaluate.py` scores them per region and share instead,
averaged over the repeats (`scores_mixed.csv`), and leaves them out of `scores.csv`,
`scores_by_class.csv` and `intervals.csv`. Within a repeat, a `mixed` plan and its `control` plan
test the same points, so their difference is a **paired** comparison: `differences.csv` holds it in
rows of kind `mixed_vs_control` (mixed minus control, per region, share and model, a block bootstrap
on the shared points, Holm-corrected across the shares of one region and model). Each arm gets its
own rows, `mixed_weighted_vs_control` and `mixed_indicator_vs_control`, corrected within its own
comparison.

**Matched leave-one-region-out draws are never pooled either.** Every draw of a basin tests the
whole basin, so `evaluate.py` scores each draw on its own and reports per basin, training size,
model and covariate group the mean and the min-max over the draws (`scores_logo_matched.csv`, as
`scores_mixed.csv` does per share). The draws are left out of `scores.csv`, `scores_by_class.csv`,
`intervals.csv` and `intervals_by_region.csv`; in `transfer.csv` they are averaged per basin under
the kind `logo_matched`; the headline metrics are `rmse__logo_m<size>_<basin>__<model>`, the mean over
the draws.

The `local` model is built to be as fair a reference as the transferred one, not a flattering one:

- **Outer folds of whole blocks.** The region's points are placed in squares of
  `bootstrap.block_size_km` (10 km), and the squares packed into `inner_cv_folds` (5) folds - fewer
  when the region has fewer squares. Each fold is predicted by a model fitted on the others, so a
  point is never predicted from its neighbours. Each local point's fold (`local_fold`) and square
  (`local_block`) are saved with its prediction.
- **Its own stage 1.** For each fold, stage 1 is refitted on the region's other folds, so the
  predicted CaCO3 and CEC it reads are local too. `local_global` reads no stage-1 prediction but
  uses the same outer folds, so both local models predict each point from the same neighbours.
- **Tuned.** Stage 2 is tuned on the same settings grid as the transferred model, through folds of
  whole squares inside the training folds.
- **Enough squares.** A region needs at least 3 squares; with fewer, every outer training fold
  would hold a single square and could neither be cross-fitted nor tuned by square. Such a region
  gets no local score, and `run_a1.py` says why.

**How different a region is.** Three measures, per held-out point and summarised per region:

- The **dissimilarity index** (DI; Meyer & Pebesma, 2021): the distance from a point to its nearest
  training point, in the model's inputs - each standardised, and weighted by how much the model
  relies on it (its feature importance) - divided by the average distance between two training
  points. 0 on top of a training point; about 1 as far away as training points are from each other.
- The **area of applicability**: a point whose DI is larger than any training point reached during
  cross-validation (leaving out outliers) is *outside* it - the model is extrapolating there.
- **MESS** (multivariate environmental similarity surface; Elith et al., 2010): for each input, how
  far inside (positive) or outside (negative) the training range a point lies, and which input is
  furthest out.

Categorical inputs are left out of all three: distances and ranges are not defined for categories.
The summary also gives each region's carbonate-class mix against training's (the *total-variation
distance*: 0 for the same mix, 1 for no class in common) and how much the SOM-CEC and SOM-CaCO₃
correlations (Spearman) differ from training's.

## Seeds: repeating the fits

`seeds` in the settings lists the random seeds (`[42]` by default; `configs/a1/a1_xgb.yml` has
`[42, 7, 2026]`).

- **The first seed alone builds the plans** - the splits, the blocks, the mixed draws. Adding a
  seed never changes a plan or its hash, so a seed can be added to a run that has already started:
  the run folder takes it, and only the new seed is fitted.
- **Every seed repeats the model fits on those plans**: the folds stage 1 cross-fits through (which
  are also the folds stage 2 chooses its settings with), the `local` model's folds, and the
  `random_state` of every estimator. The first seed's folds are the ones every run before seeds
  were repeated used; each further seed deals the points - or, on spatial and regional plans,
  whole blocks and regions - into other folds.
- **Outputs per seed**: `predictions/<plan>/seed_<seed>/` and `stage1/<plan>/seed_<seed>.parquet`;
  every prediction row has a `seed` column. `manifest.json` records the seeds done per plan, and a
  run resumes per plan and seed. `run_a1.py --seeds 42,7` runs some of the seeds.
- **The evaluation**: every table of `evaluate.py` has a `seed` column. The primary result is the
  first seed, with its block-bootstrap intervals; `seed_summary.csv` gives each headline metric's
  mean, SD, min and max over the seeds, and `differences.csv` how many seeds give each model
  difference the sign the first seed gives it (`n_seeds_same_sign` of `n_seeds`). The figures show
  the first seed.

**Where the spread comes from.** XGBoost with `tree_method: hist` and no subsampling (no
`subsample` or `colsample_*` below 1) builds the same trees from the same data whatever its seed.
The spread between seeds therefore comes from the fold assignments alone: which settings the
search picks, and which fits predict each training point's CaCO₃ and CEC in stage 1. A model fitted
on every training point with a single setting to try (`global`, untuned) gives the same predictions
under every seed.

## Round 3: the experiments that close the review gaps

Four runs, each in its own folder, then the comparisons that read across them. Nothing already
computed is rewritten: `results/a1` (TabICL) and `results/a1_mixed` (XGBoost on the processor) stay
as they are.

| # | Run | Configuration | What it answers | Folder |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | The main A1 runs, XGBoost in both stages, 3 seeds | `a1_xgb.yml` | The paper's main results without TabICL, and how far they move between seeds. | `results/a1_xgb` |
| E2 | Mixed with both arms, Oum Er Rbia and Sebou | `a1_mixed_arms.yml` | Is pooling the other basins worse than local data alone only because the local points are outnumbered (weighted), or because the model cannot tell them apart (indicator)? | `results/a1_mixed_arms` |
| E3 | Mixed with both arms, the contrast basins (Tensift, Loukkous, Bouregreg) | `a1_mixed_contrast.yml` | Does pooling help where transfer works? | `results/a1_mixed_contrast` |
| E4 | Leave one basin out, training cut to 3,600 and to 2,000 points | `a1_logo_matched.yml`, `a1_logo_matched_2000.yml` | Is a basin hard to transfer to because it is different, or because holding it out leaves less training data? | `results/a1_logo_matched`, `results/a1_logo_matched_2000` |

**`compare.py`** reads several run folders - their manifests, plan files and predictions - and
writes its tables, figures and captions into `--out`, and an MLflow run named `compare` in the
experiment of the first run. It pairs plans only on the same test points, read from the plan
files (each checked against the hash its run recorded), and stops on a mismatch. It refuses runs
fitted on different processors unless `--allow-mixed-devices`, which adds the devices to every
table; a stage a run did not fit (stage 1 of a Global-only run) does not count. The pairing keys:
basin, share and repeat for the mixed kinds; basin for `logo`; basin and draw for matched `logo`.
The model is `global` unless `--model` says otherwise; first seed, every input.

| Output | Holds | Reads |

## Prediction intervals for SOM

A model's RMSE says how far off it is on average; a **prediction interval** says, for each point,
the range SOM probably lies in. A1 gets intervals from one extra stage-2 model, `global_q`, fitted
only when `stage2.models` lists it (`a1_xgb.yml`, `a1_mixed_arms.yml`, `a1_mixed_contrast.yml`).
Without it nothing below runs, and a run writes exactly the files it wrote before.

```yaml
stage2:
  models: [global, global_aux, per_class_measured, per_class_predicted, global_q]
uncertainty:
  quantiles: [0.05, 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 0.9, 0.95]
  levels: [0.5, 0.8, 0.9]      # central intervals: 0.8 is the 10th to the 90th percentile
  conformal: true
```

**The quantile model.** `global_q` is XGBoost with `objective: reg:quantileerror` and
`quantile_alpha` set to the quantiles: one model predicts all of them, with one tree per quantile
in each boosting round (`multi_strategy: one_output_per_tree`, set for `global_q` whatever the model
list's XGBoost entry says; that entry's `multi_output_tree` is for stage 1's two targets). It reads
the same inputs as Global, on every covariate only (never per covariate group, never as the local
model). **It is not tuned on its own**: it takes the settings (tree depth, number of trees, learning
rate) that Global's search chose in the same plan, seed and arm - the weighted and indicator arms
their own Global's. So `stage2.models` must list `global` with `global_q`; loading refuses it
otherwise. The settings it took are in its `tuned_params` (`"settings_from": "global"`), its
calibration file and the plan's MLflow sub-run (`global_q_settings_from`, `global_q__<setting>`). A
run resumed after Global was saved reads them from Global's predictions file. Its fit is still
judged by the **pinball loss** (the loss that rewards a quantile for sitting where that share of
points lies below it), reported in `uncertainty_scores.csv`. The weighted arm's weights reach it as
they reach Global. Methods, in one sentence: *the quantile model uses the hyperparameters of the
tuned mean model; its intervals are conformalized, and their coverage is measured on the test
points.* On the real data it costs about 2.8 min per plan on the card (the fit and the five
calibration fits), against 1.5 min for Global's search. Each point's quantiles are sorted, since separately
fitted quantiles can cross; `quantiles_sorted` marks the points that needed it, and the share is in
the plan's log line and calibration file. The 0.5 quantile is written as the point's `predicted`
value (the median prediction); **Global, the mean model, stays the model of every accuracy result**:
`global_q` is left out of `scores.csv`, `intervals.csv`, `differences.csv`, `transfer.csv`, the
headline metrics, `compare.py` and every figure but U1-U4.

**A log-transformed SOM.** When SOM is listed in `COLUMNS_TO_TRANSFORM` (it is not in the shipped
configurations), the quantiles are fitted in log space and each is back-transformed. This is exact
for quantiles: the transform is monotone, so the share of points below a quantile is the same on
either scale, and there is no back-transformation bias (unlike a mean, whose back-transform
underestimates the mean).

**Conformalized quantile regression (CQR).** Raw quantile intervals from a model fitted to the same
points tend to cover less than they promise. CQR (Romano et al., 2019; Kakhani et al., 2024, eqs.
6-7) corrects each central interval `(q_lo, q_hi)` of nominal coverage 1 - alpha from calibration
points: each gets the score `E_i = max(q_lo(x_i) - y_i, y_i - q_hi(x_i))` (positive outside the
interval, negative inside); `Q` is the `ceil((n + 1)(1 - alpha))`-th smallest score, and the conformal
interval is `[q_lo - Q, q_hi + Q]` - wider when the raw interval covered too little, narrower when
it covered too much.

- **Calibration points.** The training points' out-of-fold quantiles: the tuned settings refitted
  once per fold of the plan's own folds, each fold predicted by the model fitted on the others. No
  training point is held back from the final model, and no point is calibrated by a model that saw
  it.
- **The calibration folds follow each design**: random folds on the random plan, 10 km blocks on
  the spatial plans, basins on leave-one-basin-out and mixed plans. In a `logo` plan, `Q` therefore
  measures the error a model makes on a training basin it did not see - the same kind of error the
  held-out basin faces.
- **Coverage is approximate, even on random splits.** `Q` comes from the fold models but is applied
  to the final model, fitted on every training point, and the tuned settings were chosen on the
  same folds. That is why coverage is always measured on the test points, never taken for granted.
- **Exchangeability.** Conformal coverage is guaranteed only when calibration and test points are
  exchangeable - drawn the same way (Kakhani et al., 2024). Under spatial blocks and leave one basin
  out they are not: the test points lie elsewhere. There coverage is **measured, not guaranteed**,
  and that measurement is a result of the experiment.

**What a run writes.** `global_q__combined.parquet` holds, beside the usual columns, `som_q05` ...
`som_q95`, the raw bounds `som_lo50` / `som_hi50`, `som_lo80` / `som_hi80`, `som_lo90` / `som_hi90`,
the conformal bounds `som_lo80_cqr` / `som_hi80_cqr` (and 50, 90), and `quantiles_sorted`. Each plan
and seed has `conformal/<plan>/seed_<s>.json` with `Q` per level, the calibration points it rests on,
the raw interval's coverage of them and the crossing shares; `Q` also goes to the plan's MLflow
sub-run (`cqr_q80__global_q`, ...). For figure U3, the random plan and the 10 km folds
(`plans.spatial_kfold.cell_size_m`, not the other block sizes) also get each test point's
dissimilarity index, in `dissimilarity_designs/` - apart from `dissimilarity/`, so the regional
summary is unchanged.

**On the graphics card.** When stage 2 is on `cuda`, `run_a1.py` also fits a tiny quantile model
there (several quantiles at once). If XGBoost cannot (an error, or its silent fallback to the
processor), `global_q` alone fits on the processor, and the manifest (`devices.global_q`), its
predictions (`device_stage2`) and its calibration files record `cpu`.

**`uncertainty_scores.csv`** (`evaluate.py`, only for a run with `global_q`), with the pooling rules of
the other tables - random pooled, spatial folds pooled per block size, plain `logo` per basin, the
mixed kinds per basin and share (the repeats' test points pooled), matched draws per draw - and per
measured carbonate class with at least `min_cell_size` test points:

| Metric | What it is |
|---|---|
| `picp` | The share of measured SOM inside the interval (prediction interval coverage probability), per level, raw and conformal, with a 95 % interval from resampling 10 km blocks. On target when equal to the level. |
| `piw_mean`, `piw_median` | The interval's width (prediction interval width), g kg⁻¹; the mean also with its interval. Narrower is better at the same coverage. |
| `qcp` | Per quantile, the share of measured SOM below it (quantile coverage probability); on target when equal to the quantile. |
| `crossing_share` | The share of points whose quantiles crossed and were sorted (one tree per quantile fits them separately: 14-15 % of held-out points on the real smoke plans). |
| `pinball` | The mean pinball loss over the quantiles: lower is better. |
| `rmse_median`, `mae_median`, `bias_median` | The median prediction's accuracy. |

MLflow gets `picp80__<scope>__global_q` and `piw80__<scope>__global_q` with their `__lo` / `__hi`
(conformal, or raw without conformal calibration), and `picp80_raw__<scope>__global_q`.

## Speed: workers and the graphics card

**Where the time goes.** One mixed plan of the real data with every model (stage 1 untuned,
stage 2 searched over 10 settings with 5 folds) took 23 minutes on the processor (an i7-9850H,
6 cores, 2 threads each). 96 % of it is XGBoost growing trees: the two per-class models 69 % (each
class searched on its own), `global` and `global_aux` 28 %, stage 1 3 %, predicting under 2 %. The
search fits 10 settings x 5 folds plus the final fit for each model - 67,100 boosting rounds for
the plan.

**The graphics card** (`stage2.init_args.device: cuda`, as in `configs/a1/a1_xgb.yml`) ran the
same plan in 14 minutes - stage 1 still on the processor - with the same RMSE for every model to
four decimals and the same settings chosen. XGBoost built without CUDA, or finding no card, would
silently fit on the processor; `run_a1.py` fits a tiny model on the card first and stops with a
message if it cannot. `run_a1.py --device cpu|cuda` puts both stages on one processor (the demo,
the tests and CI stay on `cpu`). Before predicting, each fitted model is moved to the processor,
where the data are. Two targets in one model (`multi_strategy: multi_output_tree`) do train on the
card in the installed XGBoost (3.3), 5x faster than on the processor; stage 1 stays on `cpu` in the
configurations, as it is 3 % of a plan.

**Workers.** `--workers N` runs plans and seeds in `N` processes, each estimator with
`physical cores // N` threads. The processor's second thread per core slows XGBoost down: one fit
took 7.8 s with 12 threads and 4.0 s with 6 on this 6-core machine, so a single process uses the 6
physical cores, not the 12 logical ones. The predictions are identical to one process (checked on
the demo and on real plans, on the processor and the card). The gain is modest: 10 real plans went
from 193 s to 170 s with 4 workers on the processor, and from 131 s to 111 s with 3 on the card.
Recommended: `--workers 2` on the processor. **With the card, the workers are limited by its
memory**: `--workers` at most `floor(free GPU memory / peak per process)`, where the peak per process
is the largest of any model it fits, measured with `nvidia-smi --query-gpu=memory.used --format=csv
-l 1` while one process runs a plan. Measured on the real data with `a1_xgb.yml` (every model,
`global_q` included; a Quadro T1000 with 4 GB, nothing else on it):

| Model | Peak GPU memory, one process |
|---|---|
| `global`, `global_aux` | about 0.35 GB |
| the per-class models | 0.57 GB (a leave-one-basin-out plan) |
| `global_q`, on Global's settings, one tree per quantile | 0.35 GB |

So the peak per process is about 0.6 GB, and memory alone would allow `floor(4.0 / 0.6) = 6`
processes; on a 4 GB card **use `--workers 1` with `global_q` unless the measured peak allows two**
(it does here, with room to spare). Memory is then not what limits the gain: the processes share the
card's computing, and the processor still prepares the data and fits stage 1, so `--workers 2` or
`3` is the useful range. (An earlier `global_q` that inherited `multi_output_tree` from the model
list - one tree shared by every quantile - and ran its own search peaked at 3.0 GB, which would have
allowed one process only; the measurement is worth repeating after any change to `global_q`.)

**Where the time goes, and the faster configuration.** XGBoost's `hist` method sorts each input
into at most `max_bin` value ranges (256 by default) and, for every tree level, sums the errors in
each range of each input. The time of one boosting round is therefore set by inputs x ranges x tree
nodes and hardly by the number of points: on the card, one round took 14 ms with 1,000 points and
25 ms with 100,000. Reading and preparing the data is under 2 % of a plan, so the card does not
wait on the disk; it waits on the per-round work, which is small and repeated 67,100 times.

`configs/a1/a1_mixed_fast.yml` is `a1_mixed.yml` with `max_bin: 64` in both stages, writing to
`results/a1_mixed_fast` (its plans are identical to `a1_mixed`'s). On 10 plans (5 mixed, 5 control,
regions r06 and r07, one process on the processor) it took 61 minutes instead of 152 (2.5x). The
test RMSE moved, per model, on average by:

| Model | `a1_mixed` | `a1_mixed_fast` | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| `global` | 9.46 | 9.42 | -0.04 |
| `global_aux` | 9.51 | 9.44 | -0.07 |
| `per_class_measured` | 9.47 | 9.51 | +0.04 |
| `per_class_predicted` | 9.87 | 9.99 | +0.12 |

A single plan and model moved by 0.15 on average and by 0.51 at most - the size of the change a
different seed's folds give. `per_class_predicted` moves most because the coarser stage 1 assigns
8-18 % of test points to the other carbonate class. The mixed-minus-control difference kept its
sign in 17 of 20 plan pairs and models; the three that flipped were differences of 0.2 or less,
except `global_aux` on `r06_oum_er_rbia_p30` (-0.14 before, +0.34 after). The two configurations
are different experiments: compare their scores only with each other's runs, never mixed.

Other ways to make a plan faster change the model, not only its speed, and belong in a new
configuration too: a smaller tuning grid (`params`), fewer boosting rounds (`n_estimators`, or
early stopping), or fewer inputs (fewer covariate groups).

## Watching a run

Every script reports its progress on the console as it goes, and appends the same lines to a log
file, so a run stopped half-way keeps its history:

| Script | Log file |
|---|---|
| `run_a1.py` | `results/a1/runs/<run>/run_a1.log` |
| `preflight.py` | `results/a1/preflight/preflight.log` |
| `make_plans.py` | `results/a1/plans/make_plans.log` |
| `evaluate.py` | `results/a1/runs/<run>/evaluation/evaluate.log` |
| `make_figures.py` | `results/a1/runs/<run>/figures/make_figures.log` |

To follow a long run from another terminal: `tail -f results/a1/runs/default/run_a1.log`.
`run_a1.py` writes one line when each plan starts, one for its stage 1 (time taken, and the
out-of-fold R² of CaCO₃ and CEC), one for each model and covariate group (time taken and test RMSE,
or "already done" for a resumed one), one per region and fold of the local model, and one when the
plan and seed ends, with an estimate of the time left. With `--workers`, only that last line is
shown per plan and seed (the workers' own lines go to the log file):

```text
[3/26] kfold_10km_f2 seed 42 (spatial_kfold): 4,644 train / 1,162 test
  stage 1: 41.2 s; out-of-fold R2 caco3_pct_total 0.58, c_e_c_meq_100g 0.62
  global / combined: 12.3 s, test RMSE 8.10
  ...
[3/26] kfold_10km_f2 seed 42 done in 2m14s; global test RMSE 8.10; about 41m left (2m03s per plan over 3 computed)
```

Progress and warnings go to standard error, the end summary to standard output. `--quiet` keeps only
the warnings and the summary on the console; the log file still gets every line. A `--restart`
clears the run folder but keeps `run_a1.log`. At the end of a run - finished or failed - the log is
added to the run's MLflow parent run as an artifact, and a failed run is marked `FAILED` there.

## Where the outputs appear in MLflow

Each run folder (`results/a1/runs/<run name>/`) is one **parent run** in MLflow, in the experiment
`mlflow_experiment` names (`Paper_A1`; `Paper_A1_Demo` for the demo). `run_a1.py` opens it and writes
its id into the folder's `manifest.json`; running `run_a1.py` again on the same folder reopens the
same parent, and so do `evaluate.py` and `make_figures.py`, which add their outputs to it.
`--restart` starts the folder, and its parent run, afresh.

| What | Where in MLflow | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| Settings, git commit, kinds, region and block-size choices, the SOM models and each stage's device | parent run, *Parameters* (`a1_*`, `a1_models`, `a1_device_stage1`, `a1_device_stage2`) | `run_a1.py` |
| Which models are diagnostics | parent run, tags `model_role` (each model's role) and `a1_diagnostic_models` | `run_a1.py` |
| The settings file, `metrics.csv`, `manifest.json`, `dissimilarity_summary.csv` | parent run, *Artifacts* | `run_a1.py` |
| Each plan's scores on its test points, per seed | one child run per plan and seed, `<plan>__seed_<seed>` (`plan_id`, `kind`, `held_out`, `seed` tags; `seed`, `device_stage1`, `device_stage2` parameters), metrics `rmse_test__<model>__<group>`, ... | `run_a1.py` |
| The evaluation tables (`scores.csv`, `intervals.csv`, `intervals_by_region.csv`, `differences.csv`, `transfer.csv`, `basin_errors.csv`, `scores_mixed.csv`, `scores_logo_matched.csv`, `seed_summary.csv`, ...) and `region_names.json` | parent run, *Artifacts* `evaluation/` | `evaluate.py` |
| Headline scores | parent run, *Metrics* (below) | `evaluate.py` |
| The figures | parent run, *Artifacts* `figures/` - PDF files, PNGs that preview in the browser, `captions.md`, and `data/` (each figure's numbers as CSV, the paper tables also as LaTeX) | `make_figures.py` |

**Headline metric names** are `<score>__<plans>__<model>[__<group>]`, all on test points, for
`rmse`, `mae`, `r2`, `bias` and `rpiq`:

- `<plans>` is `random`; `spatial_kfold_<size>m` - every fold of one block size pooled, e.g.
  `spatial_kfold_10000m`; `logo_<region>` - one held-out region; or `logo_mean`, the mean over the
  regions. Region names are written in lower-case letters, digits and underscores
  (`logo_oum_er_rbia`); `evaluation/region_names.json` and the parent's `region__<name>` tags give
  each one's real name.
- `__<group>` is added for a model fitted on one covariate group alone
  (`rmse__random__global_aux__terrain`) and left out for every covariate.
- `rmse__mixed_<region>_f<share>__<model>` and `rmse__control_<region>_f<share>__<model>` score
  the mixed and control plans of one region and share, averaged over the repeats
  (`rmse__mixed_oum_er_rbia_f0_1__global`); they are never pooled across shares.
- `rmse_drop__logo_<region>__<group>` is the transfer drop (`rmse_drop__logo_mean__<group>` its
  mean), and `rmse__...__lo` / `__hi` (also for `r2`) the 95 % bootstrap interval of a score on
  every covariate.
- These are the first seed's. A run of several seeds adds `<metric>__..._sd`, the SD of each over
  the seeds (`rmse__random__global_sd`).

With `--no-mlflow`, a script writes its files only. `evaluate.py` and `make_figures.py` without it
stop at once if the folder records no MLflow run (it was made with `--no-mlflow`) or MLflow no
longer has that run.
