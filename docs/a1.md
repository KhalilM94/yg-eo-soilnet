# Paper A1: soil organic matter, carbonate and transfer between regions

Paper A1 predicts **soil organic matter** (SOM, `organic_matter_g_kg`, g/kg) at the Al Moutmir
sample points from their static covariates and from percentiles of their satellite and climate time
series. It asks four questions:

- **(a) Do predicted carbonate and CEC help?** Calcium carbonate (CaCO₃, %) and cation exchange capacity
   (CEC, meq/100 g) are measured at the same points but are not available where SOM is to be
   mapped. A first model (*stage 1*) predicts them from the covariates; the SOM model (*stage 2*)
   then reads those predictions as two extra inputs.
- **(b) Does SOM behave differently by carbonate class?** The points are grouped into the four FAO
   carbonate classes - under 2 %, 2-10 %, 10-25 % and 25 % CaCO₃ or more - and the models are
   scored, and also trained, class by class.
- **(c) Does a model trained in some regions work in another?** Each of the five large hydraulic basins
   is held out in turn and predicted by a model trained elsewhere, and compared with a model
   trained on the region's own points.
- **(d) Is the loss larger where the region is more different?** The loss from (c) is set against how
   unlike its training data each held-out region is.

The code is in `src/yg_eo_soilnet/a1/`, the scripts in `experiments/a1/`, the settings in
`configs/a1/` and the tests in `tests/a1/`.

## The steps

| Step | Script | What it does | Writes, under `results/a1/` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `preflight.py` | Checks the data before anything is trained: how complete each column is, how many points each region and carbonate class holds, and how earlier CaCO₃/CEC predictions score. | `preflight/` |
| 2 | `make_plans.py` | Writes every train/test split the experiments use, once, so every model is scored on the same points (see below). | `plans/<plan>.parquet`, `plans/index.csv` |
| 3 | `run_a1.py` | For each plan: stage 1 predicts CaCO₃ and CEC; the SOM models are fitted and their test predictions saved, one row per point; for leave-one-region-out plans, how far each held-out point lies from the training data. Records everything in MLflow. | `runs/<run>/predictions/`, `stage1/`, `dissimilarity/`, `metrics.csv`, `dissimilarity_summary.csv` |
| 4 | `evaluate.py` | Scores the saved predictions: by model, carbonate class and region, with intervals, and the transfer losses. | `runs/<run>/evaluation/` |
| 5 | `make_figures.py` | Draws the paper's eight figures (also `notebooks/a1_figures.ipynb`). | `runs/<run>/figures/` (PNG and PDF) |

### The split plans

A *plan* fixes which points train a model and which score it (test). Five kinds:

| Kind | Training points | Test points | What it answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| `random` | 85 % of the points, drawn at random | the other 15 % | The baseline. |
| `spatial_kfold` | Every 10 km grid square not in the test fold | Each of 5 folds of squares in turn | How well the model does a little away from its training points. |
| `logo` | Every region but the held-out one | One whole region ("leave one region out") | (c): transfer to a region the model never saw. |
| `mixed` | Every other region, plus a share (10-90 %) of the held-out one | The rest of the held-out region | How much a little local data helps a model trained elsewhere. |
| `control` | The moved share of the held-out region alone | The same points as the matching `mixed` plan | What the other regions add, compared with local data only. |

**There is no validation set.** A model's settings (tree depth, number of trees, ...) are not chosen
on held-back validation points: they are chosen by {term}`cross-validation` *inside the training
points*. The training points are cut into 5 folds - made of whole blocks of nearby points, or
whole regions, on the spatial, leave-one-region-out and mixed plans - and each candidate setting is
scored on each fold by a model fitted on the other four. Stage 1 uses the same folds for its
out-of-fold predictions (see the leakage rule below). Since nothing is ever fitted or tuned on a
validation set, setting one aside would only take data away from training, so by default none is.
It can still be asked for - `val_size` in `plans.random` and `plans.spatial_kfold`, and
`validation_region: true` in `plans.logo` (which also covers `mixed`) - and its points are then
scored like the test points; loading such settings logs a warning saying so.

**Regions.** The regions are the 10 major hydraulic basins. Five are held out in turn - Oum Er Rbia
(2,024 points), Sebou (1,312), Tensift (1,085), Bouregreg (300) and Loukkous (290) - named in
`plans.logo.held_out`; Moulouya, Souss Massa, Daraa and Guir - Ziz - Rhris always stay in training.
A region needs at least `plans.logo.min_points` points (100) to be held out, so that its score rests
on enough points; that threshold is separate from `min_cell_size` (30), the smallest carbonate class
that gets a model of its own. Without a `held_out` list, every region with at least `min_points`
points is held out in turn - the setting to use with the finer sub-basins
(`SousBassinsHydrauliques.shp`, kept as a commented alternative in `a1.yml`). A name in the list that
is not a region of the data, or a listed region under `min_points`, stops `make_plans.py`.

**Spatial blocks.** The spatial folds are made of 10 km grid squares (`plans.spatial_kfold`,
`method: grid`, `cell_size_m: 10000`), the same size as the bootstrap's blocks. Larger blocks (the earlier 50 k-means clusters were tens of km across) push test points
further from any training point than a map pixel ever is, and make spatial CV pessimistic. To check
how much the spatial scores depend on that choice, `cell_sizes_m: [5000, 10000, 25000]` writes a
whole set of folds per size (plans `kfold_5km_f0`, `kfold_10km_f0`, ...). Each point is then tested
once per size, so the scores are always kept per size (the `block_size_m` column); `cell_size_m` is
the size the paper's spatial scores and figure 4 use.

### The models

| Model | Inputs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `global` | Every covariate | The baseline. |
| `global_aux` | Every covariate, plus stage 1's predicted CaCO₃ and CEC | Question (a). |
| `per_class_predicted` | As `global_aux`, one model per carbonate class, each point routed by its **predicted** CaCO₃ | Question (b), usable where CaCO₃ is not measured. |
| `per_class_measured` | The same, routed by **measured** CaCO₃ | A **diagnostic upper bound**, not a usable model: a new point has no measured CaCO₃. Every output marks it `model_role = diagnostic_upper_bound`. |
| `local` | As `global_aux`, trained and cross-validated inside the held-out region only, with its own stage 1 | `logo` plans with `--groups`; the reference for the transfer loss (see below). |

A carbonate class with fewer than `min_cell_size` (30) training points gets no model of its own; its points are
predicted by `global_aux`, and flagged `fallback = True`. With `--groups`, `global_aux` is also fitted
on each covariate group alone (satellite, terrain, climate, legacy soil maps, the two predictions),
to see which kind of input transfers best.

**The covariates.** The groups are in `configs/a1/covariate_groups.yml`. `landform_class` (the
terrain class, computed from the elevation model) is a terrain input, treated as a category. The
`legacy_soil` group holds layers of an existing soil map; one of them, `soc_median_0_30cm`, is an
**external estimate of soil organic carbon** - a prior on the target itself - and
`som_median_0_30cm` is the same map in organic-matter units (`soc_median_0_30cm` x 17.24). Read the
`legacy_soil` scores with that in mind: part of what the group adds is an earlier estimate of SOM.

## The leakage rule

{term}`Leakage` is a model being scored on points whose answers it saw while being built. Two
stages make it easy to introduce by accident: if stage 1 saw a point's measured CaCO₃, the CaCO₃
prediction handed to stage 2 for that point would be better than any prediction a new point could
get, and stage 2 would learn to trust it too much. The rules, each enforced by a test:

1. **Stage 1 is recomputed for every plan**, from that plan's training points only. A test point's
   CaCO₃ and CEC never enter any model of its own plan.
2. **Training points are cross-fitted.** Stage 2 is trained on stage 1's predictions for the
   training points, so those must be as good - and no better - than for a new point. The training
   points are cut into 5 inner folds, and each fold's predictions come from a stage-1 model fitted
   on the other four: no training point's prediction comes from a model that saw its measured
   value. On spatial, leave-one-region-out and mixed plans the folds are made of whole blocks or
   regions, so a point is not predicted by a model trained on its near neighbours either.
3. **Test points are predicted by the stage-1 model fitted on all the training points** - the same
   model a new point would get.
4. **Stage 2 is tuned and fitted on the training points only**, choosing its settings by
   {term}`cross-validation` over the same inner folds. Test points are only scored.
5. **The `local` model follows the same rules inside the region.** Its outer folds are whole
   spatial blocks of the region; for each fold, stage 1 is refitted on the region's other folds
   (cross-fitted by block for their points) - never the leave-one-region-out plan's stage 1, which
   was fitted on the other regions - and stage 2 is tuned on the same grid as the transferred model.

`tests/a1/test_a1_auxiliary.py` checks rules 1-3 with a *spy*: a stand-in model that records every
point it is fitted on, and fails the test if any prediction came from a model that saw that point.
`tests/a1/test_a1_run.py` checks rule 4 and that `global` never reads the stage-1 predictions.
`tests/a1/test_a1_local.py` checks rule 5 the same way, in both stages, for points and their blocks.

## Reading the results

**Intervals.** Every score comes with a 95 % interval from a {term}`bootstrap` that resamples whole
10 km squares of points rather than single points (`bootstrap.block_size_km`). Nearby samples are
alike, so resampling them one by one would treat near-copies as independent evidence and make the
intervals too narrow. Two models are compared on the same points, resampled together, which gives
an interval and a p-value for their difference. When many differences are read together, their
p-values are corrected with **Holm's method**, which raises each just enough that the chance of any
false finding in the family stays at 5 %.

**Transfer loss.** For a `logo` plan, the *drop* is the held-out region's RMSE when trained elsewhere
minus its RMSE when trained on its own points (`local`): positive means transfer does worse. For a
`mixed` plan it is compared with its `control` plan instead.

The `local` model is built to be as fair a reference as the transferred one, not a flattering one:

- **Outer folds of whole blocks.** The region's points are placed in squares of
  `bootstrap.block_size_km` (10 km), and the squares packed into `inner_cv_folds` (5) folds - fewer
  when the region has fewer squares. Each fold is predicted by a model fitted on the others, so a
  point is never predicted from its neighbours. Each local point's fold (`local_fold`) and square
  (`local_block`) are saved with its prediction.
- **Its own stage 1.** For each fold, stage 1 is refitted on the region's other folds, so the
  predicted CaCO3 and CEC it reads are local too.
- **Tuned.** Stage 2 is tuned on the same settings grid as the transferred model, through folds of
  whole squares inside the training folds.
- **Enough squares.** A region needs at least 3 squares; with fewer, every outer training fold
  would hold a single square and could neither be cross-fitted nor tuned by square. Such a region
  gets no local score, and `run_a1.py` says why.

**How different a region is.** Three measures, per held-out point and summarised per region:

- The **dissimilarity index** (DI; Meyer & Pebesma, 2021): the distance from a point to its nearest
  training point, in the model's inputs - each standardised, and weighted by how much the model
  relies on it (its feature importance) - divided by the average distance between two training
  points. 0 on top of a training point; about 1 as far away as training points are from each other.
- The **area of applicability**: a point whose DI is larger than any training point reached during
  cross-validation (leaving out outliers) is *outside* it - the model is extrapolating there.
- **MESS** (multivariate environmental similarity surface; Elith et al., 2010): for each input, how
  far inside (positive) or outside (negative) the training range a point lies, and which input is
  furthest out.

Categorical inputs are left out of all three: distances and ranges are not defined for categories.
The summary also gives each region's carbonate-class mix against training's (the *total-variation
distance*: 0 for the same mix, 1 for no class in common) and how much the SOM-CEC and SOM-CaCO₃
correlations (Spearman) differ from training's.

## Run the demo

```bash
pixi run -e dev a1-demo
```

It makes a made-up 1,200-point dataset (`experiments/a1/demo_data/`) and runs every step on it in a
few minutes, in **dev mode**: random plans, spatial folds of 10 km and 25 km grid squares, and two
of the four demo regions held out (`configs/a1/a1_demo.yml`); XGBoost in both stages, 50 trees, no
tuning, 2 inner folds, 200 bootstrap draws. It shows the pipeline works; its scores mean nothing.
Everything lands in `results/a1_demo/` (run `dev`); figure 6 says it needs mixed plans, which the
demo leaves out to stay quick.

## Run it on the real data

The real settings are `configs/a1/a1.yml`, with `configs/a1/main_config.yml` (a copy of the main
configuration whose data spec makes the soil-map layers inputs) and `configs/a1/covariate_groups.yml`.

1. **Check the paths.** In `a1.yml`, the `data:` block names the data folder and the static,
   targets and time-series files; they must match those of `configs/a1/main_config.yml` - loading
   stops if the two disagree. The regions are the major hydraulic basins, five of them held out:

   ```yaml
   regions:
     source: polygons
     polygons_path: /home/kmisbah/CESBIO/AL_Moutmir/BDMaroc/BassinsHydrauliques.shp
     name_field: Bassin
   plans:
     logo:
       min_points: 100
       held_out: [Oum Er Rbia, Sebou, Tensift, Loukkous, Bouregreg]
   ```

   (`source: column` with `column: <name>` uses a column of the data instead.)

2. **Check the data:**

   ```bash
   python experiments/a1/preflight.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml
   ```

   Read `results/a1/preflight/`: columns with many gaps, regions and region x class cells with
   fewer than 30 points, and whether the earlier CaCO₃/CEC predictions (`*_baseline`) look
   over-fitted.

3. **Write the plans** - about 2,190 plans and 235 MB, in about a minute:

   ```bash
   python experiments/a1/make_plans.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml
   ```

4. **Run the models.** Stage 1 is {term}`TabICL` in the real settings, which is slow on a CPU: it is
   fitted again for every plan and every inner fold. Start with the plans the paper's main tables need,
   then add the mixed plans:

   ```bash
   python experiments/a1/run_a1.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml --kinds random,spatial_kfold,logo --groups
   python experiments/a1/run_a1.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml --kinds mixed --groups
   ```

   A run **resumes**: stop it at any point and run the same command again, and only what is missing
   is computed. A run folder made from other settings or other plans is refused, so two experiments
   never mix; `--restart` clears it instead (as does a folder written by an older version of
   `run_a1.py`). Watch the run in MLflow (`pixi run mlflow`, experiment `Paper_A1`).

   Plans written before validation sets were dropped hold validation points; write them again
   (step 3), then run with `--restart` - the old run folders are refused, as they should be.

5. **Score it, and draw the figures:**

   ```bash
   python experiments/a1/evaluate.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml
   python experiments/a1/make_figures.py --config configs/a1/a1.yml
   ```

   or open `notebooks/a1_figures.ipynb` with the `explore` environment (`pixi run -e explore
   notebook`) and set `SETTINGS = "configs/a1/a1.yml"` and `REGIONS = None` at the top.

## Where the outputs appear in MLflow

Each run folder (`results/a1/runs/<run name>/`) is one **parent run** in MLflow, in the experiment
`mlflow_experiment` names (`Paper_A1`; `Paper_A1_Demo` for the demo). `run_a1.py` opens it and writes
its id into the folder's `manifest.json`; running `run_a1.py` again on the same folder reopens the
same parent, and so do `evaluate.py` and `make_figures.py`, which add their outputs to it.
`--restart` starts the folder, and its parent run, afresh.

| What | Where in MLflow | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| Settings, git commit, kinds, region and block-size choices | parent run, *Parameters* (`a1_*`) | `run_a1.py` |
| Which models are diagnostics | parent run, tags `model_role` (each model's role) and `a1_diagnostic_models` | `run_a1.py` |
| The settings file, `metrics.csv`, `manifest.json`, `dissimilarity_summary.csv` | parent run, *Artifacts* | `run_a1.py` |
| Each plan's scores on its test points | one child run per plan (`plan_id`, `kind`, `held_out` tags), metrics `rmse_test__<model>__<group>`, ... | `run_a1.py` |
| The evaluation tables (`scores.csv`, `intervals.csv`, `intervals_by_region.csv`, `differences.csv`, `transfer.csv`, ...) and `region_names.json` | parent run, *Artifacts* `evaluation/` | `evaluate.py` |
| Headline scores | parent run, *Metrics* (below) | `evaluate.py` |
| The eight figures | parent run, *Artifacts* `figures/` - PDF files, and PNGs that preview in the browser | `make_figures.py` |

**Headline metric names** are `<score>__<plans>__<model>[__<group>]`, all on test points, for
`rmse`, `mae`, `r2`, `bias` and `rpiq`:

- `<plans>` is `random`; `spatial_kfold_<size>m` - every fold of one block size pooled, e.g.
  `spatial_kfold_10000m`; `logo_<region>` - one held-out region; or `logo_mean`, the mean over the
  regions. Region names are written in lower-case letters, digits and underscores
  (`logo_oum_er_rbia`); `evaluation/region_names.json` and the parent's `region__<name>` tags give
  each one's real name.
- `__<group>` is added for a model fitted on one covariate group alone
  (`rmse__random__global_aux__terrain`) and left out for every covariate.
- `rmse_drop__logo_<region>__<group>` is the transfer drop (`rmse_drop__logo_mean__<group>` its
  mean), and `rmse__...__lo` / `__hi` (also for `r2`) the 95 % bootstrap interval of a score on
  every covariate.

With `--no-mlflow`, a script writes its files only. `evaluate.py` and `make_figures.py` without it
stop at once if the folder records no MLflow run (it was made with `--no-mlflow`) or MLflow no
longer has that run.
